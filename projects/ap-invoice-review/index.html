Self-initiated BA case study · 2026

Making invoice
status easier to see.

A practice case study about accounts payable visibility: map a simple invoice process, define what staff need to see, and review a small sample of invoice due dates.

Learning exercise only. The organization, process, suppliers, and invoice records are fictional; the sample is not real company or financial data.

01 / The question

What should the team
notice sooner?

In this assumed scenario, a small finance team tracks invoices in a shared spreadsheet. It is not always easy to spot which invoices are waiting for approval, which are due soon, and which may already be overdue.

The goal is not to build an ERP or prescribe accounting policy. It is to show a clear workflow, a short list of user needs, and a simple way to discuss invoice priority.

04 / User needs

Keep the requirements
easy to check.

BR-01

Find an invoice quickly

As a finance staff member, I want to find an invoice by its reference or supplier so I can check its current status.

Acceptance: a matching record shows its reference, supplier, amount, due date, and status.
BR-02

See what is waiting

As a finance staff member, I want to distinguish invoices awaiting review, approved for payment, and paid.

Acceptance: each record has one clearly displayed status from the agreed list.
BR-03

Prioritize due dates

As a finance staff member, I want to sort open invoices by due date so I can review upcoming and overdue items.

Acceptance: open records can be ordered by due date; paid records are not counted as open.
BR-04

Understand aging

As a team lead, I want a count and total amount for overdue invoices grouped by age.

Acceptance: the report shows the snapshot date, overdue buckets, invoice counts, and totals.
BR-05

Follow up responsibly

As an authorized staff member, I want a reminder list for items needing attention without automatically sending supplier messages.

Acceptance: a reminder identifies the invoice and owner; timing and recipients are confirmed with the organization first.

05 / What I learned

Start with the decision,
not the software.

The most important questions are about ownership and definitions: when is an invoice considered open, who confirms its status, and which policy determines follow-up? A useful report depends on answering those questions before choosing a tool.
